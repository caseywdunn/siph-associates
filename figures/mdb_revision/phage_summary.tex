Bacterial CRISPR systems retain fragments of viral DNA from previous
encounters. Seven matches to these fragments supported host predictions
for six viral groups. Three had \textit{Photobacterium angustum} as their
predicted host, but none of these viral groups was detected in the same
specimen as that bacterium. The other three matched other
gammaproteobacteria and were detected in the same specimens as their
respective predicted hosts. These matches link part of the viral diversity
to the associated bacterial community; spacer matches record earlier
exposure and do not by themselves demonstrate infection in the sampled specimen.
